Why it exists

Your statements already hold the answers.

Compass was built for people who'd rather not give a finance app their bank password. It works from the statement files you download yourself, so there's nothing to connect and no account to create. What it builds from them is stored in an encrypted database on your computer, and you can back it up or export it whenever you like.

No bank login

Compass never asks for one, and it has no feature that connects to a bank. You get the statement from your bank's own website and import the file yourself.

Rules you can see working

Built-in rules sort common merchants as you import. When one lands in the wrong category, fix it and save the fix as a rule. Before you save, a live preview shows which of your recent transactions the rule matches, and saving can sort the ones still uncategorized.

Yours to keep

Your data is a database file on your own disk, encrypted with SQLCipher, and you can take a full backup or export your transactions as CSV whenever you want. Compass is source-available under the PolyForm Strict License, so you can read exactly what it does with your money.

How it works

From statement file to sorted spending, in three steps.

01

Download a statement file

Sign in to your bank or card issuer's website as usual and download your transactions as a CSV file, or as an Excel workbook if that's what they offer. Look for an export or download option near your account activity. Compass never sees that login.

Bank and credit card statements import as CSV or Excel (.xlsx). Compass also reads investment exports from Wells Fargo Advisors, Fidelity and Thrivent, plus Principal 401(k) and E*TRADE / Morgan Stanley PDF statements. Support varies by institution and statement type.
02

Import it and check the mapping

Open Import, pick the statement type and choose the file. Compass suggests which columns hold the date, description and amount. You confirm them, check which way the money moved and look at the preview before anything is imported. Save the layout, and the next file from that bank goes quicker.

03

See where it went

Built-in rules sort common merchants into categories. Fix any that land in the wrong place and save the fix as a rule for next time. Then open the Dashboard, Trends and Budgets to see where the month went, or Plan to see what's coming.

Demo Mode loads sample data, so you can look around before you import a thing.

Features

Everything you need. Nothing you don't.

A personal finance companion for your desktop: it reads your bank statement CSVs, sorts the spending, and shows you the month ahead.

CSV and Excel Import

Bring in bank and credit card statements as CSV or Excel (.xlsx) files. Compass suggests the column mapping. You get a preview before anything is imported. Saved layouts make the next file from that bank quicker, and duplicate detection checks each import against what's already there. Batch import takes several files at once.

Category Rules

Built-in rules categorize common merchants on import. Correct a category and you can save the correction as a rule, so future imports apply it. When you write your own, a live preview shows which recent transactions match before you save, and saving can sort the ones still uncategorized.

Dashboard & Trends

Monthly income, expenses, and net savings at a glance. 3, 6, or 12-month trend charts showing income vs. expenses and per-category breakdowns. Navigate any month freely, past or future.

Budgets & Goals

Soft monthly spending limits with on-pace projections so you can catch a potential overspend before month end. Track net savings, spending limits, and income targets as goals with progress bars.

Ghost Subscription Detector

Finds recurring charges with the exact same description and amount appearing in 2 or more months. Lists monthly and estimated annual totals for every subscription you may have forgotten about.

Insights & Privacy

Automatic spending analysis surfaces budget warnings, top merchants, detected subscriptions, paycheck timing, and overdraft alerts, all processed locally. SQLCipher AES-256 encrypted database. No telemetry.

Security & Privacy

Questions you should ask before trusting any finance app.

Compass keeps your financial data in an encrypted database on your computer. Encryption protects that file at rest. It can't protect a computer someone else controls, or a copy of the database that travels with its key, so the answers below say exactly where things live.

Do I have to connect Compass to my bank?
No. Compass has no live bank connections and never asks for a bank login. You download statements from your bank or card issuer as CSV or Excel (.xlsx) files and import them, then import a newer statement whenever you want to catch up.
Where is my data stored?
On your computer, in the app data folder: %APPDATA%\com.compass.app\ on Windows or ~/Library/Application Support/com.compass.app/ on macOS. That folder holds the encrypted database, compass.db, and a key file, compass.key. There's no cloud sync.
Is my data encrypted, and what does that cover?
Yes. Compass encrypts its database at rest with SQLCipher (AES-256). The key is kept in Windows Credential Manager or the macOS Keychain, with compass.key in the app data folder as a fallback. That key file can decrypt the database, so protect both files and every backup of them. Encryption at rest isn't a substitute for securing your device.
How do I back up my data, and can I share the backup?
Use Settings > Backup & Restore > Export Backup to write a .compassbackup file. Don't share it: it contains the database and the key that decrypts it, so store it as carefully as the statements themselves. For a plain copy of your transactions, export them as CSV from Transactions, one profile at a time.
Does Compass connect to the internet?
Yes, to check GitHub for new releases, and to deliver feedback when you press Send feedback. Your transactions aren't uploaded, and there's no telemetry or cloud sync. Once it's installed, Compass works offline.
What does the Send feedback button send?
Only what you type, plus two technical details: your message, a reply email if you add one, the Compass version and your operating system go privately to the developer when you press Send, and Compass attaches nothing else, so your transactions and other financial data stay on your computer.
Can the developer see my financial data?
Not through Compass. There's no Compass account, cloud sync or bank connection, and the app doesn't send your transactions to the developer. Your data is only as private as the computer it's on, so protect the device, the key file and your backups.
